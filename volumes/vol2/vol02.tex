\part{Silicon Horizon}
\label{part:silicon-horizon}

\begin{volumesintro}
\lettrine[lines=3]{A}{rrays exist} in physical hardware. Volume I already established why a transistor can hold one bit and how bits compose into Boolean algebra; this volume starts exactly there and builds upward---from composed logic gates, up through memory cells, processors, caches, and the massively parallel engines---multicore CPUs, vector units, and GPUs---that move and transform arrays today. This volume builds that complete picture, one layer at a time, continuing directly from where Volume I left off, without re-deriving what Volume I already covered.

\vspace{1em}
\textbf{What Makes This Different:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Silicon to Software:} Complete vertical integration, continuing directly from Volume I's voltage-to-bits foundation
    \item \textbf{Modern Architecture:} Multi-core, SIMD, GPU, and heterogeneous systems treated as natural extensions of single-unit hardware
    \item \textbf{Performance Foundations:} Why code runs fast or slow, at every scale
    \item \textbf{Hardware-Software Contract:} What each layer assumes, and what arrays can rely on in Volume III
\end{itemize}

\begin{quote}
\textit{``The purpose of computing is insight, not numbers.''}

\hfill--- \textsc{Richard Hamming}
\end{quote}
\end{volumesintro}
